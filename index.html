The main thing I'm building

GridCast — electricity demand, forecast out loud

Electricity grids need tomorrow's demand to plan fuel, storage and imports. Most student projects plot history. GridCast issues a forecast for 10 series across 8 grid operators — Kazakhstan, Great Britain, Ireland, France, Germany, Belgium, Denmark, Australia — and keeps score of itself against the official numbers when they arrive.

Under the hood: ingestion from 10 public operator APIs, a leak-proof backtesting protocol (a feature cannot use data that wasn't published yet), four model families from a naive baseline to Amazon's zero-shot Chronos, conformal prediction intervals, and statistical significance tests — applied against itself, with the losses published too.

How I work

The part admissions can actually verify.

Adversarial review, every stage

Every build step faces an independent reviewer whose job is to prove it should not ship. It caught silent history-deletion in reingest, a naive model that only looked accurate because of a publication lag, and a healthcheck that would have blocked its own dashboard. Each fix has a regression test. The log is public.

No invented numbers

Every claim on the site comes from a JSON the pipeline wrote, stamped with the exact git SHA and a dirty-tree flag. When significance testing weakened one of our headline results, the dashboard was corrected to say so.

Small team, organization habits

One developer, but with CI gating every commit, a self-updating data pipeline, per-market health checks, incident postmortems, and a documented benchmark card instead of a cherry-picked chart.
